### R2-04 — Error `type` is called an 'origin-relative error identifier' while RFC 9457 defines it as a URI reference — implementers will split on resolving vs. matching

**Section:** §7.1 · **Sources:** Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI) (convergence 1) · **Verdict:** fold_corrected

**Triage reason:** Real ambiguity introduced by the round-1 P7 fold itself, so it is not 'already addressed': citing RFC 9457 imports that spec's URI-reference semantics, and 'origin-relative error identifier' does not say whether a client resolves against the descriptor URL, resolves against the origin, or treats the token as opaque. Proven on the wire: the reference worker emits `type: '/say/v0/e/off'`, `'/say/v0/e/bad-job'`, `'/say/v0/e/bad-urgency'`, `'/say/v0/e/method'` — origin-relative paths that are identifiers to match on, never documents to fetch. One clause closes it without changing a byte anyone serves.

**Verification record:** Anchor verified: patch_current appears exactly once (line 167). The defect is real and not addressed elsewhere — §6's descriptor table (lines 136-141) never mentions errors, and §7.1 line 167 is the only place `type` is described, so "origin-relative error identifier" is the whole of the guidance. But the literal patch text has two problems against the wire and against RFC 3986, so it goes in corrected. (1) FALSE DISTINCTION: `/say/v0/e/bad-job` is an absolute-path reference; under RFC 3986 §5 it resolves to the identical absolute URI whether the base is the descriptor URL or the origin root, since an absolute-path reference discards the base path entirely. "Resolves it against the ORIGIN, not against the descriptor URL" tells an implementer to look for a difference that cannot exist, and then contradicts itself two clauses later by forbidding dereference — if it is never dereferenced, the resolution base is moot. (2) IMPLEMENTATION CONFLICT, and it is the sharper one: the patch pins `type` to the full path form and gives `/say/v0/e/bad-job` as the example, one sentence before the untouched "The descriptor declares the error identifiers an origin can emit." On the wire, descriptor.ts:90 declares BARE tokens — `errors: ['off', 'bad-job', 'bad-urgency', 'method']` — while preflight.mjs:59 emits `type: \`/say/v0/e/${type}\``. So the patch as written makes a client read the descriptor entries as full paths and string-compare them to `type`, which fails on every error the reference surface serves. The candidate's own example creates a second split of exactly the kind it was folded to close. Corrected text keeps the finding's substance (match, never dereference), drops the phantom resolution base for the accurate "absolute-path reference," and states the descriptor-token-to-wire-path relation the reference surface actually serves. Confirmed the "never a document" half is true on the wire: worker.mjs routes only ASK_PATHS (`/say/v0/ask`, `/agent/v0/preflight`); nothing is served under `/say/v0/e/`. No settled law is touched — grain-lock, §12, §7.6, §16 and HTTPS are untouched, and the site-declared-vocabularies law is strengthened rather than eroded, since the correction is what makes the declared error vocabulary usable.

**Wire notes:** descriptor.ts:90 declares the error vocabulary as bare tokens (`errors: ['off','bad-job','bad-urgency','method']`), while preflight.mjs:59 and worker.mjs:30,60 emit the wire value as `/say/v0/e/<token>`. The proposed text pins `type` to the full path form immediately before the sentence saying the descriptor declares those identifiers, which would have a client compare descriptor entries verbatim to `type` and miss on all four. Also RFC 3986: an absolute-path reference resolves identically against the descriptor URL and the origin, so "against the ORIGIN, not against the descriptor URL" names a distinction with no wire consequence.

A signature proves who signed, never that the claim is true — a cryptographically signed self-attestation is still a self-attestation, and §3 already rules that self-badge claims without a validator profile are non-conformant marketing. It also imports key management into a v0 that deliberately defers signatures entirely (§3 L2 'unsigned allowed in v0', §8, §11 key lifecycle out of scope per §20b item 6), and it would put a compliance-attestation burden on exactly the small operators §2 exists to keep in. This finding is the load-bearing half of Qwen's BLOCK vote; the editors should record that the block rests on a remedy the charter forecloses.

**R2-28** (§10; Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI)) — Parity is invoked normatively but the validator and walk are unspecified.
already addressed: round-1 ARGUE B3, ruled and countersigned at §20b item 5 ('parity called as a validator / walk property with normative ship expectation'), and written into §10's second paragraph. gpt-oss-120b filed the identical structural point three ways in round 1 and the editors decided it explicitly rather than by silence: parity is a validator property, not a wire property, the Water Heater Walk is named in §21, and the one wire-level test anyone proposed (machine answer as a verbatim subset of a phone call) was declined at round-1 DECLINE 16 because it would make `as_of`, `gen`, `doc`, and the anti-redlining `basis` all non-conformant. Kimi is right that the walk is not yet specified in a document; that is §19 gate 3 work in the validator lane, not a missing spec clause.

**R2-29** (§7.1; Llama 4 Scout (Meta)) — An unknown or undeclared `need` member MUST return an error.
Contradicts a round-1 fold: §7.1's `need` rule (§20a batch 1 item 4, GLM-5.2) pins unknown members to be IGNORED and named back in the response 'so divergent implementations cannot silently split', and the reference surface implements it (`need_unknown` in the yes body, with `need_encoding` declared in the descriptor). Llama's patch would invert that into a hard error, which reintroduces the split the fold closed and makes a consumer's optimistic request for a lean field fatal to an otherwise answerable question. Adjacent to round-1 DECLINE 11, where Nemotron's object-shaped `need` was declined for colliding with the same fold. The legitimate half of Llama's finding — that the descriptor must DECLARE the vocabulary — is folded at R2-01.

**R2-30** (§7.6; Qwen3 30B (Alibaba)) — Safety referrals MUST carry a JSON-LD link to the official emergency page plus a verification timestamp and a `safety_unverified` status.
already addressed: §7.6's referral-veracity rule (round-1 P12, §20a batch 3 item 12) is the converged fold of round 1 — the referral MUST name the public emergency service for the jurisdiction, MUST be what the issuing authority itself publishes, MUST NOT be the origin's own sales or affiliate line, and the descriptor declares it in advance so an agent can check it before an emergency rather than during one. Qwen's added machinery also breaks a settled rule and a physical fact: §16 requires action pointers to be same-origin or `tel:` URIs, which a JSON-LD link to a third-party authority page is not, and most emergency authorities publish a NUMBER rather than a stable machine-readable page. A `safety_unverified` status that triggers on 'malicious or outdated referrals' presumes a verification oracle v0 has no mechanism to consult.

**R2-31** (§6; Qwen3 30B (Alibaba)) — Cap the `jobs` array at 1000 entries and add a `job_count` field.
Contradicts the editors' ruling against unsourced numbers: §20b item 7 / round-1 B5 held change-feed caps at RECOMMENDED because picking a figure with no deployment data is exactly the vibes §15 forbids, and 1000 is that figure with no data behind it. The premise is also wrong on the measured wire: §15 records the descriptor at 617 tokens for a 12-job vocabulary, fetched once per agent, and notes 'the weight IS the job vocabulary, which is what the agent came for' — the cost scales with the thing the consumer is buying. A `job_count` field additionally duplicates the length of an array the consumer already holds.

**R2-32** (§5.2; Mistral Small 3.1 (Mistral AI)) — New deployments MUST (not SHOULD) use `/say/v0/*` and `ask`.
Contradicts §5.2's own frame and the §20b item 2 ruling: the conventional layout is declared 'informative as a starting point; the descriptor remains authoritative once found', and the very next sentence requires that 'Descriptors MUST list the paths they actually serve'. A MUST on the path would make the descriptor's authority conditional and would break the discovery order the same section defines, where the link relation comes first and the conventional path is only a fallback. The wire-token alignment was ruled jointly by both editors and countersigned in full; the SHOULD is what makes the legacy tolerance in the same paragraph coherent.

**R2-33** (§7.1; Mistral Small 3.1 (Mistral AI)) — A client MUST resend with a corrected job after a 400.
Out of scope and wrong in kind: the sentence Mistral patches states the ORIGIN's duty (return the whole job vocabulary in one round trip) and 'teach in one round trip' describes what that buys the caller. Mandating that the client then resend makes a consumer that reads the vocabulary and correctly concludes the site does not do this job non-conformant, which is the opposite of the cheap-refusal thesis. v0 constrains what an origin serves and what a consumer may CONCLUDE (§7.2's unknown-`r` rule, §7.1's 'an error is not a `no`'); it does not compel a consumer to make a second request.

**R2-34** (§10; Mistral Small 3.1 (Mistral AI)) — `noindex` should be MUST rather than SHOULD.
already addressed: round-1 ARGUE B2, ruled and countersigned at §20b item 4 ('`noindex` remains SHOULD (hosts that cannot set headers); anti-cloak remains MUST'), and §10 now carries the reasoning in its own text — the SHOULD exists so shared hosts that cannot set headers are not manufactured non-conformant, and the anti-cloak MUST is the rule that actually protects anyone. gpt-oss-120b asked for the same escalation in round 1. Noted for the record that the reference surface sends `x-robots-tag: noindex` on every response including errors, so the fleet already does voluntarily what the MUST would compel.

**R2-35** (§16; Mistral Small 3.1 (Mistral AI)) — Add an HSTS requirement alongside HTTPS.
Buys nothing the round-1 HTTPS fold has not already bought, and repeats the header problem it was written around. §16 (P16, converged by two round-1 models) places the duty on the CONSUMER: a surface reachable only over plain HTTP is ABSENT, not degraded, and its answers MUST NOT be reported as Say answers. That closes the downgrade path at the only point v0 can enforce, without a header a whole class of honest shared hosts cannot emit (§20b item 4 / B2). HSTS remains ordinary good hosting practice and needs no protocol clause to be worth doing.

**R2-36** (§15; Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI)) — Naming `o200k_base` binds the standard to one proprietary tokenizer.
already addressed: round-1 disposition DECLINE 23, filed there by three models (gpt-oss-120b, Nemotron, GLM) and answered the same way. §15 is informative, scopes `o200k_base` as the reference measurement rather than a requirement, and already instructs implementers to 'publish yours with the number'. `o200k_base` is openly published in tiktoken, not proprietary. Naming the tokenizer IS the method-not-vibes rule, and it is a signed §22 amendment. Four models across two rounds have now asked for a change the current text already makes, which suggests the sentence could be read more easily than it could be improved.

**R2-37** (§14; Llama 4 Scout (Meta)) — Make the complaint door mandatory with a defined resolution process.
already addressed: round-1 ARGUE B6, ruled and countersigned at §20b item 8 ('complaint schema waits for a walked door'). §14 is explicitly conditional ('If offered'), the reference implementation declares `complaint: null`, and pinning a schema plus a resolution process for a door nobody has walked through is the publisher-only-artifact failure the whole protocol was designed against — the same reasoning that made P7 fold a PROVEN error shape rather than an imagined one. Llama's patch would additionally make every L1 origin owe a complaint-handling process, which is an operations mandate no wire format can test.

**R2-38** (§2; Llama 4 Scout (Meta)) — The protocol says nothing about a site whose human-visible substance is itself deceptive.
already addressed: §10's anti-cloak MUST binds the machine answer to the human substance, and round-1 disposition DECLINE 18 answers the residue in one line — 'A business lying about its own staffing is fraud, and no wire format has ever prevented one.' Parity is a relation between two surfaces of one origin; when both lie in the same direction the protocol has kept its promise (nothing is asserted to a machine that a human could not obtain) and the wrong is one the law, not the wire, addresses. §6's credential rule already closes the one case a spec CAN close: no pointer to the issuing authority's own check surface, no credential claim.

**R2-39** (§masthead; Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI)) — 'Say' is too close to 'SayVel'; competitors will read the protocol as vendor capture.
already addressed: the masthead's normative non-affiliation clause ('Say is not SayVel. Implementing Say MUST NOT require a SayVel account, Agent Doors, or any vendor relationship. Conformance is behavior plus validator, never a purchased badge'), the product split paragraph above it, §20b item 1 which put that clause in the mast on purpose, and §18's licensing table plus the closing 'Being copied is success'. The name itself is locked by the Captain (2026-08-11) and is a publication-authority decision under §19 gate 1, not an editors' fold. Kimi's underlying observation — that the disclaimer alone may not convince a competitor — is a real adoption risk answered by second implementations and a neutral public home (§19 gate 6), not by spec text.

**R2-40** (§20b, 22; Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI)) — Strip the §20b ARGUE commentary and the §22 ratifying-models row before publication.
Contradicts the document's own published-record commitment. §20 promises structured critique with 'rejections recorded' and §20a states plainly that 'The ratification invitation promised that rejections would be recorded in public; that file is the record'; the round-1 disposition file exists for the same reason and keeps its own stale closing line on purpose rather than tidying it after the fact. The drafting-room material Kimi would move to commit messages is the evidence that the normative text was argued rather than asserted, which is the strongest thing a spec authored by model crews can offer a skeptical reader. Kimi is right that a frozen v1 would separate normative text from process record; that is a §19 gate 6 packaging decision, not a deletion.

**R2-41** (§7.3, 12; Llama 4 Scout (Meta)) — Add more `no` examples and more guidance on what counts as a regulated vertical.
§7.3 already carries six worked refusal examples plus the two rules that make a `no` conformant (grain present on geographic refusals and omitted on others, no marketing CTA), and the vocabulary is site-declared, so additional examples in the spine would model a vocabulary no site is required to share. On §12, the verticals are named explicitly (housing, lending, insurance underwriting, employment, healthcare benefit determination) alongside the protected characteristics added by round-1 P15, and further guidance on where a business's obligations begin is legal advice sitting behind §19 gate 5 — the same reason B7's legal-sounding paragraph was held for counsel rather than folded. No patch was offered and none is anchorable.

**R2-42** (§9; Qwen3 30B (Alibaba)) — §9 should specify a maximum change-feed retention period.
already addressed: round-1 ARGUE B5, ruled and countersigned at §20b item 7 ('change-feed caps stay RECOMMENDED (no vibes numbers)'). gpt-oss-120b asked for the same thing in round 1 and the answer stands: RECOMMENDED is RFC 2119 working as designed, and picking 30 days or 100 entries with no deployment data is the vibes §15 forbids. A git-backed fleet's natural feed length is its commit history, which is not a number the spec gets to choose for other people.

**R2-43** (§general; Mistral Small 3.1 (Mistral AI)) — The protocol depends on validators that may not be widely available or trusted.
True and already accounted for rather than defective: §3 makes validator profiles the difference between conformance and self-badging, §1 commits the validator as one of the three things shipping alongside the format, §19 gate 3 withholds publication until measured walks exist from a real deployment, and §21 names the Water Heater Walk. The concern is the same family as R2-20 (publisher-only rot) and is carried by the roadmap. No patch was offered; nothing in the wire changes on either answer.

**R2-44** (§7.5; Qwen3 30B (Alibaba)) — A healthcare provider could return `indeterminate` for some conditions while keeping human-only triage.
already addressed: §12 excludes healthcare benefit determination from conformant use outright, and §7.5's folded rule (round-1 P11) forbids `indeterminate` for anything the site can reach a `no` from using its own declared facts. Beyond that, the scenario describes a provider keeping a human in the loop for clinical triage, which §2.6 endorses rather than forbids — 'human consent stays human where law and decency require it'. Round-1 DECLINE 18 answers the residue: a business misrepresenting its own clinical capability is fraud, not a wire defect. The §2.4 fail-closed citation is a misread; fail-closed governs UNCONFIGURED surfaces returning clean failures, not verdict selection.

**R2-45** (§6; Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI)) — `rate` has no pinned JSON type.
already addressed: §20a batch 1 item 6 turned `rate` into a DECLARED POSTURE explicitly including an honest "none", and round-1 disposition DECLINE 10 rejected gpt-oss-120b's fixed `{ limit, window_seconds }` object for contradicting that fold — shared hosting cannot always meter, and a declared absence beats a fictional limit. Pinning a single shape is precisely the change the editors ruled against six days ago. If the round-2 concern is that a consumer cannot tell a posture string from a posture object, that is covered by the same descriptor-declares-its-vocabularies discipline folded at R2-01 and can be trued there without reopening the posture ruling.
